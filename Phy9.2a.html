Core concept
Two types of wave

Transverse and Longitudinal

Transverse = across. Longitudinal = along.

Learn
  • A transverse wave has a direction of vibration that is perpendicular to the direction of wave motion.The particles go across the wave. Picture the rope: the wave heads for the wall, and each bit of rope goes straight up and down.
  • A longitudinal wave has a direction of vibration that is parallel to the direction of wave motion.The particles go along the wave. Picture the spring: the wave heads away from you, and each coil shuffles forwards and backwards in that same line.
  • Light, infrared radiation and water waves are transverse.
  • Sound waves are longitudinal.
Key Facts
  • Transverse: vibration perpendicular to wave motion. Light, infrared, water waves.
  • Longitudinal: vibration parallel to wave motion. Sound.
  • Sound is the one to remember. It is the only longitudinal example in this syllabus.

Core concept
Parts of a wave

Characteristics of a Transverse Wave

There are 5 different ways to describe a wave.

Learn
  • The crest is the highest point of a transverse wave, and the trough is the lowest point.
  • Displacement is the distance of the particle away from the rest (or undisturbed) position.How far from home the particle is right now. It changes all the time as the wave goes past.
  • Amplitude is the maximum displacement from the rest position!! 
    It is the height of the crest or trough.The biggest the displacement ever gets. Measure from the middle line up to a crest, not from a trough all the way up to a crest.
  • Wavelength is the shortest distance between any two identical points which are in phase. 
    It is the length of one complete wave.
    In PHASE?

    “In phase” means moving together at exactly the same stage of a repeating motion.

    For waves, two points are in phase when they:

    • have the same displacement from the rest position, and
    • are moving in the same direction at the same time.

Key Facts
  • Amplitude is measured from the rest position, not from trough to crest. Trough to crest is twice the amplitude.
  • Wavelength (λ), pronounced lambda, is the length of one complete wave.
  • Phase: any two points on the wave which move in the same direction and have the same speed and displacement from the rest position. They are exactly one wavelength away.

Practice: answer, then say why

The amplitude question that catches almost everyone.

Answer, then say whyTwo tiers
1Pick an answer, pick the reason, then say how sure you are.

A buoy rises 1.5 m from its lowest point to its highest point as waves pass. What is the amplitude of the wave?

Tier 1 — what happens
Tier 2 — why it happens
Open the three-view model. Drag P to a crest and read its displacement, then drag it to a trough. The two readings are equal and opposite, and each one is the amplitude. Add them together and you have the 1.5 m the question gave you.
Check the direction of the fix. Trough to crest is bigger than the amplitude, so the answer must be smaller than 1.5 m, not larger.
The cycle is not the issue. Amplitude is a distance on the vertical axis, measured from the middle line.
✦Beyond the syllabusAmplitude is not loudness

Not examinable. Worth knowing anyway.

NOT EXAMINABLE · PURELY DELICIOUS

Amplitude is not loudness, and it is not brightness. For sound, doubling the amplitude does not double the loudness you hear: your ear works roughly logarithmically, which is why decibels exist. For light, the amplitude sets the brightness but the frequency sets the colour, and no amount of amplitude will turn red light blue.

Core concept
Wavefronts and graphs

Wavefronts

The distance between two wavefronts is the wavelength of a wave.

Learn
  • A wavefront is an imaginary line that joins all the adjacent points that are in phase.Join up all the points that are doing exactly the same thing at the same moment, and you have drawn a wavefront. On water, the line of a crest is the easy one to see.
  • The distance between two wavefronts is the wavelength of a wave.
  • A straight dipper gives plane wavefronts; a spherical dipper gives circular wavefronts.The shape of the dipper decides the shape of the lines. A long bar pushes a whole line of water at once, so the wavefronts come out straight.
Key Facts
  • A wavefront joins points in phase, not simply points at the same height.
  • Wavefront to wavefront is one wavelength.
  • Waves travel at right angles to their own wavefronts.

Wavefronts and graphs

Reading a displacement graph

Transverse waves can be represented using a displacement-distance graph.

Learn
  • A particle above the rest position has a positive displacement; below it, negative.
  • At a crest the displacement is a maximum and positive. At a trough it is a maximum and negative.At the very top and the very bottom the particle is as far from home as it ever gets. That distance is the amplitude, and only the sign changes between them.
  • At the rest position the displacement is zero, and the particle is moving fastest.This one catches people out. Zero displacement does not mean stopped. It is the moment the particle is racing through the middle, like a swing at the bottom of its arc.
Key Facts
  • At a crest or a trough the displacement is the same size as the amplitude.
  • Zero displacement does not mean the particle is stationary. It is moving fastest there.
  • The horizontal axis is distance, so this graph is a photograph of the whole wave at one instant.

Example 4: particle paths

The trick for direction is the same every time: copy the particle behind.

Example 40 / 8
1Two steps ask you to choose before they tell you.

A wave is moving to the right. Five particles are marked. Describe the full path taken by particles P, Q and R in one complete oscillation, then state one similarity and one difference between particles S and T.

wave direction: rightP at a crestS below restT above rest
  1. One full cycle: away, back through the rest position, out the other side, and back again. It ends exactly where it started.
  2. P is at the crest, so it starts at maximum positive displacement.
  3. For a wave travelling right, each particle copies the one just behind it, on its left. Behind the crest the rope is lower, so P goes down.
  4. Maximum positive displacement → rest position → maximum negative displacement → rest position → maximum positive displacement.Say every stage. A mark scheme wants the direction and both extremes.
  5. Q is on the rest line and, by the same rule, is moving downward.Rest position → maximum negative displacement → rest position → maximum positive displacement → rest position.Same journey, started at a different point in the cycle.
  6. R is at maximum negative displacement and can only go up.Maximum negative displacement → rest position → maximum positive displacement → rest position → maximum negative displacement.All three particles trace the same path. They just start at different points on it.
  7. S and T are both on the part of the wave that rises as you go right, so both are moving downward.
  8. Similarity: both are moving in the same direction (downwards). Difference: S has a negative displacement and T has a positive displacement.A similarity about motion and a difference about position. Do not give two of the same kind.
