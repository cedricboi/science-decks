Core concept
Same medium

Same medium

The medium will determine the speed of the wave always.

Learn
  • Wave speed is constant for the same medium.Shaking the rope harder or faster does not make the wave travel faster along it. How quickly a wave moves is a property of the rope, not of your hand.
  • When frequency changes, the wavelength will also change.Because v = f lambda and v cannot move, f and lambda have to trade off. Double the frequency and the wavelength halves.
  • This follows from v = f λ with v held fixed.
    • Explained

    Think of the medium as a speed limit for the wave. While the wave stays in the same medium, it travels at the same speed.

    Making waves more often does not make them travel faster. It only packs them closer together.

    Same medium = same wave speed. Higher frequency = shorter wavelength.

    The speed changes only when the medium changes, for example when a wave goes from air into water.

Key Facts
  • Same medium: v fixed. So f up means λ down.
  • The source sets f. The medium sets v. λ is whatever is left over.
  • This is why a ripple tank dipper on a faster setting gives more closely spaced wavefronts.

The frequency of a ripple tank dipper is doubled. The water depth is unchanged. What happens?

Core concept
Crossing a boundary

A different medium

The one quantity that cannot change is the one the source controls.

Learn
  • Frequency is constant when a wave moves into a different medium.Think of crests arriving at the boundary. If sixty arrive each second, sixty must leave each second. Otherwise crests would pile up at the boundary forever.
  • The wave speed changes because the new medium is different.Light slows down in glass. Water waves slow down in shallow water. Sound speeds up in water compared with air.
  • Since v = f λ and f is fixed, the wavelength must change in the same proportion as the speed.
Key Facts
  • Different medium: f fixed, v changes, so λ changes with it.
  • Slower medium means a shorter wavelength.
  • Same medium: v fixed. Different medium: f fixed. Learn which is which.

Practice: Example 10, direction, f and T

The direction rule is worth more marks across a paper than either calculation.

Example 100 / 6
1Two steps ask before they tell.

The diagram shows a water wave travelling to the right at 2.0 m/s. State the direction of motion of P and of Q, then find the frequency and the period.

v = 2.0 m/sλ = 8.0 mwave travels right
  1. For a wave travelling right, each particle copies the particle just to its left. Look at the rope immediately to the left of the particle: if it is lower, the particle moves down.
  2. To the left of P the rope is higher, so P moves upward... check the diagram carefully: to P's left the curve is lower, so P moves downward.
  3. Q moves upwardQ is on the opposite slope from P, so the same rule sends it the other way.
  4. Hz
    λ = 8.0 m, so f = v / λ = 2.0 / 8.0f = 0.25 Hz
  5. T = 1 / f = 1 / 0.25T = 4.0 sPeriod is always one divided by the frequency.
  6. A wave 8 m long moving at 2 m/s takes 4 s to pass any point. That is exactly the period, so the two answers agree with each other.

Practice: Example 11, counting wavefronts

Count the wavelengths first. Most lost marks here are miscounts.

Example 110 / 6

Circular wavefronts spread out from a point source P. P to the first wavefront is half a wavelength, and measures 0.30 m. The waves take 0.875 s to reach Y. 


(a) Calculate the wavelength. (b) Hence calculate the wave speed.

P to first wavefront = 0.30 mthat distance is ½λtime P to Y = 0.875 s
  1. 1(a) What is 0.30 m the length of?
    It is the distance from P to the first wavefront, and the question tells you that this is half a wavelength.
  2. 2(a) So find the wavelength
    m
    λ = 2 × 0.30λ = 0.60 m
  3. 3(b) How far is P to Y?
    P sits half a wavelength before the first wavefront, and Y is on the fourth wavefront. Each further wavefront adds one whole wavelength.P to Y = ½λ + 3λ = 3.5λ
  4. 4(b) Turn that into a distance
    d = 3.5 × 0.60 = 2.1 mThe frequency is not given, so the route is v = distance / time.
  5. 5(b) Evaluate the speed
    m/s
    v = 2.1 / 0.875v = 2.4 m/s
  6. 6Is the magnitude sensible?
    2.4 m/s across waves 0.60 m long means four waves pass every second. That is a brisk ripple, which is what a point source in a tank produces.
Note. The printed stem calls the 0.30 the gap between two wavefronts. That gap is a whole wavelength, which would give 0.30 m, not 0.60 m. It is P to the first wavefront, and its unit is missing.

Pulling it together

Which is fixed, which is free?
1Tap each card to reveal what happens to that quantity.
Same medium: the speed Fixed. The medium owns it.
Same medium: the frequency Free. The dial sets it.
Same medium: the wavelength Free. It follows the frequency.
New medium: the speed Changes. New medium, new speed.
New medium: the frequency Fixed. Crests cannot pile up.
New medium: the wavelength Changes with the speed.
✦Beyond the syllabusWhy you hear round corners but cannot see round them

Not examinable. Worth knowing anyway.

NOT EXAMINABLE · PURELY DELICIOUS

This is why you can hear round a corner but not see round one. Sound in air has a wavelength of about a metre, which is the same size as a doorway, so it spreads out through it. Light's wavelength is half a micrometre, millions of times smaller than the doorway, so it goes straight through in a beam. Same physics, same equation, wildly different wavelength.
